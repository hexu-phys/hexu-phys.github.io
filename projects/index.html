Overview

My work is theoretical soft matter: the statistical mechanics of classical many-body systems, field- and mean-field descriptions of collective behaviour, and the numerical work needed to test those descriptions. Each project below starts from a reduced model of the collective degrees of freedom, derives what that model predicts, and checks the prediction against a simulation or against measurements on colloidal systems.

Availability. Simulation and analysis code for these projects is not public; where a line reads [Code available upon request], I can share a description or a private walkthrough on request and with my advisor's approval. The write-ups linked below are working drafts shared for discussion, not peer-reviewed publications.

Current projects

  • Configuration field theory

    Nov 2026 – Present · Draft report

    The Gaussian universality of a configuration network is normally a statement about finite matrices, their traces, and their cumulants. This report rewrites it as a statement about fields: configurations on a hypertorus, an operator-valued field of elementary moves, and the spectral measure of the integrated operator. In that language the same criterion becomes a percolation statement, and the path integral and the Wilsonian flow of ordinary field theory become available for configuration space problems.

  • Gaussian universality of configuration space networks

    Sep 2026 – Present · Ongoing

    The connectivity graph of a many-body configuration space often has the same spectrum in the thermodynamic limit: across frustrated magnets, one-dimensional lattice gases, and spin glasses, the empirical spectral distribution of the adjacency matrix approaches a Gaussian. With Yilong Han I am isolating the sufficient conditions for that universality, formulating the problem in operator algebra and controlling the moments through a decay assumption on cumulative traces. A paper is being prepared for submission to Physical Review X.

  • Rotational Langevin dynamics of a coupled disk array

    Oct 2026 – Present · Ongoing

    In a dense two-dimensional array of disklike colloids the centres are fixed but each disk can rotate, and neighbouring disks are coupled through the lubrication flow in the narrow gaps between them. I derive the effective rotational Langevin equation for this system, analyse its mode structure and equal-time correlations beyond the mean-field estimate, extend the treatment to weakly disordered lattices, and compare the predicted correlations with measurements on disk arrays fabricated by photolithography by Yifan Huang (PhD student) in Prof. Yilong Han's lab.

Approach

  • Start from a reduced description of the collective degrees of freedom: mean-field theory where it is controlled, a field theory where it is not.
  • Use the free energy as the default route into phase behaviour, linear stability, and pattern formation, and treat symmetry as the organising principle behind the spectrum of a model.
  • Treat numerical work as a test of the analytic prediction rather than a substitute for it; the parameters always come from the physical system, not from what makes the fit easy.
  • Compare with experiment wherever data exist, which for colloidal systems means measured correlation functions and structure factors rather than single-particle trajectories.
  • Keep the analysis reproducible: one entry point per project, and every figure produced by a script.
