Overview

My work is theoretical soft matter: the statistical mechanics of classical many-body systems, field- and mean-field descriptions of collective behaviour, and the numerical work needed to test those descriptions. Each project below starts from a reduced model of the collective degrees of freedom, derives what that model predicts, and checks the prediction against a simulation or against measurements on colloidal systems.

Availability. Simulation and analysis code for these projects is not public; where a line reads [Code available upon request], I can share a description or a private walkthrough on request and with my advisor's approval. The write-ups linked below are working drafts shared for discussion, not peer-reviewed publications.

Current projects

  • Phase-space networks with a universal Gaussian spectrum

    Sep 2026 – Present · Ongoing

    The connectivity graph of a many-body phase space often has the same spectrum in the thermodynamic limit: across frustrated magnets, one-dimensional lattice gases, and spin glasses, the empirical spectral distribution of the adjacency matrix approaches a Gaussian. I am isolating the sufficient conditions for that universality, formulating the problem in operator algebra and controlling the moments through a decay assumption on cumulative traces.

  • Rotational Langevin dynamics of a coupled disk array

    Oct 2026 – Present · Ongoing

    In a dense two-dimensional array of disklike colloids the centres are fixed but each disk can rotate, and neighbouring disks are coupled through the lubrication flow in the narrow gaps between them. I derive the effective rotational Langevin equation for this system, analyse its mode structure and equal-time correlations beyond the mean-field estimate, extend the treatment to weakly disordered lattices, and compare the predicted correlations with experimental measurements.

Approach

  • Start from a reduced description of the collective degrees of freedom: mean-field theory where it is controlled, a field theory where it is not.
  • Use the free energy as the default route into phase behaviour, linear stability, and pattern formation, and treat symmetry as the organising principle behind the spectrum of a model.
  • Treat numerical work as a test of the analytic prediction rather than a substitute for it; the parameters always come from the physical system, not from what makes the fit easy.
  • Compare with experiment wherever data exist, which for colloidal systems means measured correlation functions and structure factors rather than single-particle trajectories.
  • Keep the analysis reproducible: one entry point per project, and every figure produced by a script.
