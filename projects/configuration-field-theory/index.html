Research question

The companion paper proves that the spectrum of the configuration network of a lattice system is Gaussian, by a moment computation on a finite matrix. That statement is exact but tied to the lattice: the objects it uses are finite matrices, their traces, and their cumulants.

The question this report asks is whether the same result can be written as a statement about fields. If a configuration is a field on a torus and an elementary move is an operator-valued field, then the adjacency matrix becomes an integrated operator on the configuration space, and the finite-matrix notions become a tracial state, a spectral measure, and cumulants of the move field. Once the statement is in that language, the standard machinery of statistical and quantum field theory becomes available for configuration space problems.

Approach

The report develops a dictionary between three languages: linear algebra on a finite space, operator algebras, and a field theory of configurations. Four steps carry it.

  • The configuration field theory itself. Fields live on a hypertorus, the elementary moves form a local operator-valued field, and the integrated move is both a vector field on the configuration manifold and an operator on the configuration Hilbert space. Translation covariance Φ(r + a) = U(a) Φ(r) U(a)−1 makes the operator translation invariant, so it is fibred over the Brillouin zone.
  • Gaussian spectral laws. The normalised trace becomes a tracial state, the eigenvalue distribution becomes a spectral measure, and the finite-matrix cumulants become partition-lattice cumulants of the move field. The Gaussian criterion and its variance then take a field-theoretic form, with the variance given by the integral of the connected two-point cumulant.
  • The theorem as a diagnostic. Read in the contrapositive, the criterion becomes a percolation statement. A soft-core calculation shows how the range of the interaction controls the crossover between the Gaussian and non-Gaussian regimes, with soft spheres and jamming as the test case.
  • Dynamics and coarse graining. The same field is then read dynamically: a Kolmogorov propagator and a time-sliced path integral give an action functional, and the entropy is read off from that action. A momentum-space decomposition, the local differential operator, and a Wilsonian flow extend the treatment to spatially correlated cases, with one-loop diagrams as the first correction.

The lattice results and their proofs are collected in the companion paper; this report states them in configuration-space language and refers to the paper for the estimates.

My contribution

  • Set up the three-way dictionary between finite matrices, operator algebras, and the configuration field, including the translation-covariant move field and its integrated operator.
  • Rewrote the Gaussian criterion as a statement about the cumulants of that field, and derived the percolation reading and the soft-core crossover from it.
  • Worked out the path-integral entropy and the momentum-space Wilsonian flow, and wrote the report.
