Research question

The phase space of a many-body system grows exponentially with its size, yet the density of states of its connectivity graph keeps coming out the same. Across a family of frustrated and glassy models, the empirical spectral distribution of the phase-space adjacency matrix converges, after a suitable normalisation, to a Gaussian in the thermodynamic limit, largely independent of microscopic detail.

The question is which structural properties are actually responsible: which assumptions are sufficient for the limit to hold, and how the limiting variance is fixed by the two-point correlations of the model.

Approach

Rather than treating each graph geometry as a separate problem, I formulate the question in operator algebra so that the graph models share one language, and then control the moments of the spectrum with an assumption on the decay of cumulative traces. Four steps carry the argument.

  • An exactly solvable reference case. For a periodic lattice gas of commuting local spin-flip operators the configuration graph is a hypercube, whose spectrum is a sum of independent Rademacher variables: the Gaussian limit follows from the classical central limit theorem, and fixes both normalisation and shape of the answer.
  • Locality. Replacing the hypercube by distant local operators whose cluster commutators decay exponentially in the diameter of their support, the same limit survives, with a variance determined by the two-point correlations.
  • Conserved charges. When the model has a symmetry, the adjacency operator is block diagonal in the charge sectors. Each sector inherits a Gaussian limit, and the full spectrum is a mixture that collapses to a single Gaussian exactly when the sector variances concentrate.
  • Coarse graining. The limit is stable under a decimation-type coarse graining: the coarse-grained network obeys the same law up to a deterministic rescaling, which is the precise sense in which the self-similar structure seen in earlier numerical work appears.

The hypotheses are then checked against the models that motivated the study, a frustrated spin glass with conserved energy and a lattice gas with conserved particle number, where the interactions are strictly local and no further estimate is needed.

My contribution

  • Set up the operator-algebra formulation that treats the graph models in one framework rather than case by case.
  • Developed the moment argument behind the limit theorem, including how conserved charges and coarse graining enter.
  • Carried out the numerical spectral checks on the test models and the low-dimensional toy model used to illustrate the locality hypothesis.
