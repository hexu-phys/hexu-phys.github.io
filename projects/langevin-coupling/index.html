Research question

In a dense monolayer of colloidal disks the centres are jammed by the neighbours, but every disk can still rotate, and the fluid squeezed into the narrow gaps transmits torque between neighbours. Those rotational degrees of freedom are usually dropped from collective descriptions of such a layer.

The question is what the coupling does to the collective rotational dynamics: what the effective damping of a mode is, how the equal-time correlations fall off with distance, and whether the answer survives the disorder of a real array.

Approach

  • Effective equation. Disks on a triangular lattice, in the lubrication limit where the mean gap is much smaller than the radius and the flow in the gap is a creeping Stokes flow, exchange a torque proportional to the sum of the two angular velocities. The angular-momentum balance for each disk then closes into one rotational Langevin equation with an effective damping, a nearest-neighbour coupling, and a stochastic torque, with the coupling coefficient estimated from the gap geometry and the viscosity.
  • Beyond mean field. The mean-field damping is only the uniform limit of the mode structure. A Fourier analysis on the triangular lattice gives the damping of every mode and, after inverse transform, the real-space correlations, which are not monotonic in distance.
  • Stationary correlations. The single-mode variance is obtained exactly, and the subtraction of the mean from the measured correlation is treated explicitly, since that subtraction is what sets the stationary kernel and the shell correlations an experiment can actually extract.
  • Weak disorder. The lattice description is carried over to a weakly disordered medium through a disorder-averaged structure factor, so that the damping and the equal-time correlations can be predicted for an array whose spacing is not perfectly periodic.
  • Experiment. The prediction is written in terms of correlation functions and structure factors that can be measured directly, and compared with observations on colloidal disks.

My contribution

  • Derived the effective rotational Langevin equation, including the lubrication torque between neighbouring disks and the coefficient estimate.
  • Worked out the mode analysis beyond the mean-field damping, the stationary correlation, and the disordered generalisation.
  • Carried out the numerical evaluation and verification, and set up the comparison with the experimental data.
